Part B · Stakeholder engagement: Medibank's feedback

How our partner's view moved across the project. Use this to show the team listened and changed the solution because of it.

"Don't know"→"This is actually going to work"
PM1 · Week 1
The problem
Medibank told us non-resident OSHC customers are declining. The product's features are good but students don't discover them, and students leave it to their agents.

Source: Partner Meeting 1 team notes (internal), summarised in Charles's A2 reflection.

PM3 · Week 5
The concern
Medibank was worried that posting on Chinese social media would run into regulation.

Source: Partner Meeting 3 team notes (internal). Background: Internet Advertising Measures 2023 (China News); Internet Insurance Business Measures 2020, Arts. 3, 23.

Our response
What we changed
We made "inform, never sell" a design rule: no prices, quotes, comparisons or purchase links, sponsored posts labelled 广告, and every post goes through a compliance check (22 rules) before it can be published.

Source: references/compliance.md in the agent repository. Details are shown in Part 4.

Final meeting · PM4
The ask
Kimi wanted the AI agent to produce results directly: find specific creators to work with, and create ready-to-post social media content.

Source: final partner meeting notes (internal).

Our response
The shift
We rebuilt the agent around outputs. It now finds real creators (28 Xiaohongshu creators reviewed, 8 shortlisted) and writes complete posts, image cards and short videos. This moved the partner from "don't know" to "this is actually going to work".

Source: creators/scouted/xiaohongshu.csv; agent repository. Shown live in Part 4.

Key message

The problem is trust and visibility, not the product.A1 + PM1.
The right channel is peers on Xiaohongshu.A2 + A3.
Every round of partner feedback changed the solution.PM3 → compliance check; PM4 → agent that finds creators and writes content.

Part A. Why social media

A1. Medibank never talks to Chinese students directly

  • ICEF Monitor (2019), PRISMS data: https://monitor.icef.com/2019/10/education-agents-refer-75-of-australias-international-students/ → 73% of Chinese students were referred by education agents → the agent usually arranges OSHC, so Medibank has no direct relationship.
  • Medibank & Nous (2026) p. 32, n = 2,296: https://www.medibank.com.au/content/dam/overseas/pdfs/Medibank-Nous-From-Surviving-to-Thriving-Report-2026.pdf → under 1 in 5 international students understand the health system well; only 39% of students with poorer wellbeing know what OSHC doesn't cover (vs 54%) → students hold a policy they don't understand.

A2. Students trust peers far more than brand ads

  • Digital Business Lab (2025), n = 510 HK/SG: https://www.marketing-interactive.com/singapore-consumers-trust-peers-over-brands-on-xiaohongshu → 46.6% trust regular Xiaohongshu users vs 22.9% brand accounts (KOLs 30.3%) → a brand account alone is the least trusted voice.
  • Medibank & Nous (2026) pp. 23, 26 → peers among students' most trusted sources; report recommends equipping peer networks → Medibank's own study supports a peer-led approach.
  • Nielsen (2021): https://www.nielsen.com/ja/news-center/2021/trust-in-advertising-study-reveals-real-life-situations-and-humour-as-the-top-attributes/ → 88% trust friends and family → supporting evidence only.

A3. Xiaohongshu is where students search

  • IT之家 (2024): https://www.ithome.com/0/817/514.htm → ~70% of monthly users search; ~90% say results affect purchases → it works like a search engine.
  • iClick (2026): https://kr.i-click.com/iclick-news/xiaohongshu-insightshow-to-capture-the-evolving-chinese-study-abroad-market-in-2026/ → study-abroad audience 84.2% female, 78.6% aged 18–34; daily-life content performs best → content should look like daily life.
